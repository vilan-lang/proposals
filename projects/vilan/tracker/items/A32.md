# A32 — custom signals with compatibility: `Source` is the trait, and the question is its reach

- status: OPEN — owner-proposed 2026-08-29 ("should Signal become a
  trait?"); reframed on the finding that the trait already exists
- kind: design
- area: Reactive core & UI
- source: the owner's item, 2026-08-29; kolt's StorageSignal the live exhibit

The ask: custom signal logic (a persisted signal, a debounced one, a
computed view) implementable while staying compatible with everything that
consumes signals. The finding: **std already has the readable trait** —
`Source<T>` (`get`/`sub`/`effect`-default, reactive.vl ~429), charter
verbatim "code that only observes can't tell a local signal from a remote
one" — and kolt's `StorageSignal<T>` implements it TODAY. What remains is
design, not invention:

- **Reach** — the consumers are the gap, not the trait (A33's audit: the
  UI bindings take concrete `Signal`).
- **Combinators** — `map`/`combine`/`flatten` live on `Signal`;
  `RemoteSource::map` exists separately. Generic combinators over any
  `Source` would unify the three worlds (and meet B157's `MaybeSignal`
  vocabulary — one family of bounds, not three).
- **The write side** — is a `Sink<T>`/writable trait wanted (`set`/
  `set_with`), or does writing stay concrete? kolt's StorageSignal
  duplicates the write surface by hand today. Bindings that WRITE BACK
  (`bind_value`, `bind_draft`) are the consumers that would bound on it.
- **Whether `Signal` the struct stays the one canonical cell** (my lean:
  yes — the trait is the compatibility surface; the struct is the std
  implementation; "Signal becomes a trait" in spirit, with the name
  `Source` already minted and shipped).
