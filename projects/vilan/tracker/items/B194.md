# B194 — `[derive]` on a generic type generates ERASED impls

- status: OPEN — b188 lane's STOP (2026-09-01); a ruling and a reflection-surface change
- kind: design
- area: Type system & the type solver
- source: lane b188 (the derive generators spell a subject by its bare name in every role)

`[derive(Wire)]` on std's own `struct Handle<T>` (arena.vl:23) emits
`impl Handle with Json`, `Result<Handle, str>`, `fun rebuild(...):
Handle` — under-supplied applications that B188's erasure was
exactly what let type-check. B188's arity refusal is therefore GATED
on `source_id != DERIVED_SOURCE`: total over what a program WRITES,
exempt for what a generator writes. Closing the exemption is three
things: (1) the macro reflection surface (`macro_std/src/meta.vl`'s
`StructItem`/`EnumItem`) carries NO generic parameters — add them;
(2) a derived impl over a parameter needs BOUNDS nothing computes
(`impl Box<type T: Json> with Json`) — rule the bound synthesis (the
derived trait on every parameter is the obvious default, Rust's
rule); (3) the Rust fallback generator mirrors it. Until then a
derive over a generic type is unsound in the same way B188 was.

## 2026-09-01 — a second exemption this item lifts

Lane b174 (the generic-LEFT operator refusal) hit derive-generated
code as a third surface: `[derive(PartialEq)]` on a generic struct
emits an `eq` comparing a `T`-typed field, which the new rule refuses
wholesale — so b174 exempted `DERIVED_SOURCE` on B188's boundary,
both halves pinned. B194's bound rule (the derived trait required on
every parameter → `T: PartialEq` present) makes that emitted `==`
legal on its own terms; when B194 lands, lift BOTH gates (B188's and
b174's) in one stroke and re-run their pins.

## Coupling 2026-09-01 (lane b174's find)

B174's rule refuses an operator on an unbounded generic left operand;
`[derive(PartialEq)]` on a generic struct emits `eq` over a `T` field,
so b174 exempted the derive surface on B188's `DERIVED_SOURCE`
boundary (pinned). When B194 lands, THREE gates lift — B188's arity,
B189's covered set, b174's operator exemption — and four estate
sites take the bound: `generics.rs:5460`, `tuples.rs:131`,
`borrows.rs:1072`, `std/src/arena.vl:23`.

## STOPPED at the ruling's boundary 2026-09-01 (lane b194) — OWNER'S WORD NEEDED

The ruled default (bound EVERY parameter under the derived trait,
Rust's rule) breaks C7's shipped, pinned, documented doctrine: std's
`Handle<T>` is a PHANTOM-parameter subject, `[derive(Wire)]` on it
would produce `impl Handle<type T: Wire> with Wire`, and
`Handle<Session>` becomes un-sendable — against the pin "a name is not
the thing it names" (borrows.rs:1041), cells.md:105, services.md
§naming layer, and `is_wire_type`'s own comment.
MEASURED AMENDMENT (not enacted): bind a parameter under the derived
trait iff the generated body REACHES it (it appears in some
field/payload type); a phantom gets a bare binder `type T`. Exact and
syntactic in vilan (a phantom parameter appears nowhere — unlike
Rust's `PhantomData<T>`, which is why Rust cannot do this). 919/919
across macros+borrows+generics+tuples incl. the C7 pin. Both
directions necessary: the bare binder alone refuses `self.value
.debug()`; the derived-trait bound alone breaks `Handle<Session>`.
RECOMMENDATION: adopt the reachability rule — it IS the ruled default
for every parameter the body touches and differs only where the ruled
default contradicts C7. Family reclassified FEATURE (nothing runs
wrong today: phantom derives round-trip correctly under the erasure;
reachable ones are refused at compile time).
Also found: for `Wire` the derived-trait bound is pure cost
(`is_wire_type` still refuses `Pair<T> { value: T }` at the
declaration) — two mechanisms would disagree; `Handle::from_json`
launders a `Handle<str>` payload into a `Handle<i32>` (inherent to a
phantom name from text; C7's per-arena brand is the belt).
