The paper answers "is per-struct signal colouring a principle of reactivity?" (§1: no — the irreducible part grows with shapes and operators, not user types) and lays out the three layers. Revision 1's Q1 (§4.2): a COLD `derive` runs per READ, not per change, so a per-run owner there releases one reader's resources on another reader's pull — ruled (C), refuse and steer to `derive_memo`; revision 2's R29 dissolves the problem instead: a pipe has no readers, only its one consumer (`[resource]` + `own self` makes a second consumer a compile error). Costs: no unsubscribed pull read (`.sample()` replaces it), a struct holding a pipe becomes move-only (B460 gains value), and the move-only guarantee needs B463 fixed or `own self` everywhere.

Slices S1–S7 (§12): S1 the `Flow`/`Pipe` split and sealing (breaking; kolt migrates; B463), S2 per-run owners on every pipe stage, S3 transient sources, S4 flow-following collection operators, S5 boundary conversions (`.coll_by(key)`/`.coll()`), S6 tracked reads (needs B458), S7 the `Store` paper.
