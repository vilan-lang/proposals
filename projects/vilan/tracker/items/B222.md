# B222 — a guard ending in `panic(..)` does not bind the continuation: B187's decision is made during the walk, B204's divergence leaves are settled after it

- status: OPEN — found at Order 26's integration (2026-09-03) when B187's `#[ignore]`d pin was un-ignored after B204 landed and still failed
- kind: bug
- area: Type system & the type solver

B187 declares an else-less negated `is` guard's captures into the
enclosing scope when `Divergence` says the then-block diverges — and
it asks during `walk_expr_node` (analyzer.rs ~22032). B204 made
`panic(..)` (and the endless `for`) leaves of that same walk, but the
leaves are computed in `resolve_world` (~35760), AFTER the body walk,
because a call's subject is not known until names resolve. So at the
moment the guard is walked, `panic("missing")` is not yet a leaf and
`if !(maybe is Some(let n)) { panic("missing"); } print(n)` refuses
`n`. `ret`/`jump` guards work (shape-readable leaves). Fix: decide the
continuation binding after the leaves settle — record the candidate
(the false-path capture set + the then-block) at walk time, and
declare it into the scope in a pass that runs once `divergence_leaves`
exist and before the constraint fixpoint reads the scope (the same
place B204 settles the leaves is the natural seam). Pin: the existing
`b187_a_panicking_guard_binds_the_continuation` (un-ignore), plus an
endless-loop guard, plus a control that a non-diverging guard still
binds nothing.
