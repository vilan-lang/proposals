# M185 — No way to decode, resize and encode an image: a server can't make smaller copies of an upload

- status: OPEN - filed 2026-11-02 (kolt)
- kind: feature (std; M)
- area: Standard library

Kolt wants custom avatars (kolt task #9): someone uploads a picture, and the server keeps a few smaller copies of it (say 32, 64 and 256 pixels square) so pages load only what they show. Kolt already stores uploads; what it can't do is turn one into a smaller one. std has no image module: nothing decodes PNG, JPEG or WebP into pixels, scales or crops them, or encodes the result.

The same gap blocks thumbnails of pictures sent in messages (a 4,000-pixel photo is shown at 300) and a resized preview for the image viewer. Today an app's choices are a native dependency it would have to wrap per platform (`sharp` on node, which ships prebuilt binaries per OS and architecture, and has no counterpart on the native backend), or sending full-size pictures and letting the browser scale them.

Recommendation: a `std::image` that works on both backends:

- `Image::decode(bytes: Bytes): Result<Image, str>` for PNG, JPEG, WebP and GIF (first frame), honoring JPEG's EXIF orientation, with the decoded size checked against a pixel limit before allocating (an upload can claim 60,000 x 60,000).
- `image.width()`, `image.height()`, `image.resized(width, height, fit)` with `Fit::Contain | Cover | Fill` and a good downscaling filter (Lanczos or area averaging, not nearest), and `image.cropped(x, y, w, h)`.
- `image.encode(format, quality): Bytes` for PNG, JPEG and WebP.

On node it could sit on a WebAssembly build of a small codec set, or on `sharp` where it's present; natively, Rust's `image` crate covers all of it. Sizing M, mostly in choosing the codecs and keeping both backends' output close.
