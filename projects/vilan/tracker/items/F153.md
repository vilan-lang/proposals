# F153 — Natively, `for x in subject` over a `[resource]` struct whose inherent `next(self): Option<T>` drives the loop MOVES the subject into the loop's iterator, and the analyzer's last-use drop after the loop then names the moved binding - cargo refuses the emitted Rust (E0382, "use of moved value") where JS runs it (the resource drops after the loop) - so `for key in keys` over `process::keys()` builds on JS only

- status: OPEN - filed 2026-10-26 (std-55b's find, Order 55)
- kind: native backend defect (cargo refuses) - a for-in over a resource
- area: Native targets & rendering

Met verifying terminal.md Q6 for A173 S2 (`for key in keys` through the implicit `next`, F-2/F-3 fixed in Order 54). The plain (non-resource) awaiting inherent `next` works on both backends (verified); the RESOURCE form does not: `vilan_rust::Emitter::for_each_iterator` emits `let mut iterator = <subject>;` through `consumed_value_of`, while the analyzer treats the loop as a LOAN of the resource and places its `Drop` after the loop (`std::mem::drop(ticks)`), so the binding is used after its move. The fix is the loop borrowing a place subject that is a resource (`let iterator = &mut ticks;` and `next(iterator, ..)`), with the binding emitted `mut`; a temporary subject (`for k in keys()`) still moves. Repro: res_for.vl (raw on/off prints, a `jump break` loop and a full loop).
